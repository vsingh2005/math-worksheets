\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
    \large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Foundation: Dilations \& Scale Factor}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
Unlike translations, reflections, and rotations, a \textbf{dilation} is NOT a rigid motion because it changes the size of the figure. However, it preserves the shape. 

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Scale Factor ($k$):} The ratio of the lengths of the image to the corresponding lengths of the pre-image.
    \item \textbf{Enlargement:} If $k > 1$, the figure gets bigger.
    \item \textbf{Reduction:} If $0 < k < 1$, the figure gets smaller.
    \item \textbf{Coordinate Rule:} When dilating from the origin $(0,0)$, multiply both $x$ and $y$ coordinates by the scale factor: $(x, y) \rightarrow (kx, ky)$.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{4mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Applying Dilations}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
We can find the coordinates of a dilated figure by multiplying by the scale factor.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Worked Example 1: Finding the Image}

\medskip

\textbf{Problem:} A triangle has vertices at $X(2, 4)$, $Y(4, 6)$, and $Z(6, -2)$. It is dilated by a scale factor of $\dfrac{1}{2}$ with the center of dilation at the origin. What are the new coordinates?

\medskip

\textbf{Solution:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Rule:} $(x, y) \rightarrow (\dfrac{1}{2}x, \dfrac{1}{2}y)$
    \item $X'(2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2}, 4 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2}) = (1, 2)$
    \item $Y'(4 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2}, 6 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2}) = (2, 3)$
    \item $Z'(6 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2}, -2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2}) = (3, -1)$
\end{itemize}

\end{tcolorbox}
