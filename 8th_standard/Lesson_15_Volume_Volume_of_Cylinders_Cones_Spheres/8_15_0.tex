\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
    \large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Foundation: Volume Formulas}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
\textbf{Volume} is the amount of 3-dimensional space an object occupies. We use $V$ for volume, $r$ for the radius of the circular base, and $h$ for the height of the object.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cylinder:} A prism with a circular base. Formula: $V = \pi r^2 h$
    \item \textbf{Cone:} Comes to a single point. It fits perfectly into a cylinder with the same base and height, but it only takes up one-third of the space! Formula: $V = \dfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$
    \item \textbf{Sphere:} A perfectly round 3D ball. Formula: $V = \dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3$
\end{itemize}

\vspace{4mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Calculating Volume in Terms of Pi}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Often, you will be asked to leave your answer "in terms of $\pi$". This means you treat $\pi$ like a variable and don't multiply it out using $3.14$.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Worked Example 1: Volume of a Cylinder}

\medskip

\textbf{Problem:} Find the volume of a cylinder with a radius of $4\text{ cm}$ and a height of $10\text{ cm}$. Leave your answer in terms of $\pi$.

\medskip

\textbf{Solution:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Step 1 (Write Formula):} $V = \pi r^2 h$
    \item \textbf{Step 2 (Substitute values):} $V = \pi \cdot (4)^2 \cdot 10$
    \item \textbf{Step 3 (Square the radius):} $V = \pi \cdot 16 \cdot 10$
    \item \textbf{Step 4 (Multiply numbers):} $V = 160\pi\text{ cm}^3$
\end{itemize}

\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{4mm}
