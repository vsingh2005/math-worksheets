\vspace{5mm}

\noindent This worksheet challenges your understanding and application of linear equations and systems. You will encounter multi-step word problems, complex algebraic manipulations, and conceptual questions requiring deep insight. Success requires not just computation, but also strategic problem-solving, careful equation formulation, and clear interpretation of results. Remember to:
\begin{itemize}[nosep, leftmargin=0.8cm]
    \item \textbf{Define Variables:} Clearly state what each variable represents.
    \item \textbf{Formulate Equations:} Translate word problems into precise mathematical expressions.
    \item \textbf{Solve Systematically:} Apply algebraic properties (distributive, combining like terms, inverse operations) with precision.
    \item \textbf{Check Solutions:} Verify your answers, especially in word problems, to ensure they make sense in context.
    \item \textbf{Explain Reasoning:} For conceptual questions, articulate your understanding clearly and concisely.
\end{itemize}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Advanced Equation Solving \& Real-World Modeling}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1.5cm, leftmargin=0.6cm] 
\item A rental car company offers two deals. Deal A: $\$30$ per day plus $\$0.15$ per mile. Deal B: $\$50$ per day with unlimited free miles. If you rent the car for 3 days, set up an equation to find exactly how many miles you would need to drive for the costs to be equal. Solve it.
\vspace{3cm} 

\item Solve the complex equation for $x$. Show every step clearly: 
$$ \frac{2}{3}(6x - 9) + 4 = -\frac{1}{2}(4x + 8) + 12 $$
\vspace{3cm} 

\item Two competing internet providers, "ConnectFast" and "StreamMax", offer different pricing structures. ConnectFast charges a $\$75$ activation fee plus $\$45$ per month. StreamMax charges a $\$30$ activation fee plus $\$50$ per month. After how many months will the total cost for both providers be the same? What will that total cost be?
\vspace{3cm} 

\item A rectangular swimming pool has a perimeter of $120$ feet. The length of the pool is $10$ feet more than twice its width. Set up an equation to find the dimensions of the pool. Then, calculate the area of the pool.
\vspace{3cm} 

\end{enumerate}

\item Three siblings have ages represented by consecutive integers. The sum of their ages is 45. Let $x$ be the youngest sibling's age. Set up an equation and find the ages of all three siblings.

\item A chemist needs to mix two solutions. Solution A is 20\% acid, and Solution B is 60\% acid. She wants to create 15 liters of a 35\% acid solution. How many liters of Solution A and Solution B should she use?

\item A plane travels at a constant speed. It takes 3 hours to fly 1800 miles with a tailwind. On the return trip against the same wind, it takes 4 hours. What is the speed of the plane in still air, and what is the speed of the wind? (Hint: Let $p$ be plane speed, $w$ be wind speed. Set up a system of two linear equations.)

\end{enumerate}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Conceptual Understanding \& Interpreting Solutions}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1.5cm, leftmargin=0.6cm, resume] 
\item Explain the geometric meaning of "infinite solutions" when comparing two linear equations. If you graph the left side of the equation as $y_1$ and the right side as $y_2$, what does the graph look like? Provide an example of such an equation.
\vspace{4cm} 

\item A rectangular garden has a length that is $4\text{ feet}$ less than 3 times its width. If the perimeter is $64\text{ feet}$, construct an equation, solve for the width, and find the exact dimensions and area of the garden.
\vspace{3cm} 

\item Consider the equation $A(x-B) = C(x+D)$. For what values of $A, B, C, D$ would this equation have:
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=0.5cm]
        \item Exactly one solution?
        \item No solution?
        \item Infinitely many solutions?
    \end{enumerate}
    Provide a specific numerical example for each case.
\vspace{5cm} 

\item A company's profit $P$ (in thousands of dollars) from selling $x$ units of a product can be modeled by the equation $P = 0.5x - 150$.
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=0.5cm]
        \item How many units must be sold for the company to break even (profit is $\$0$)?
        \item If the company wants to make a profit of $\$50,000$, how many units must they sell?
        \item Explain what the $-150$ in the equation represents in a real-world context.
    \end{enumerate}
\vspace{4cm} 

\end{enumerate}

\item A piggy bank contains quarters, dimes, and nickels. There are twice as many dimes as quarters, and three more nickels than dimes. If the total value of the coins is $\$12.75$, how many of each coin are there?
\vspace{4cm} 

\item Two trains are 400 miles apart and traveling towards each other on parallel tracks. One train travels at 50 mph, and the other travels at 70 mph.
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=0.5cm]
        \item How long will it take for the trains to meet?
        \item How far will each train have traveled when they meet?
    \end{enumerate}
\vspace{4cm} 

\item Solve the following equation for $x$:
$$ \frac{3}{4}(8x - 12) - \frac{1}{3}(9x + 6) = 5x - 10 $$
\vspace{4cm} 

\item A cylindrical tank is being filled with water at a rate of 5 liters per minute. The tank initially contains 20 liters of water. A second, identical tank is being filled at a rate of 7 liters per minute but starts with only 10 liters of water.
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=0.5cm]
        \item Write an equation for the volume of water in each tank after $t$ minutes.
        \item After how many minutes will the two tanks contain the same amount of water?
        \item What will be the volume of water in each tank at that time?
    \end{enumerate}
\vspace{4cm} 

\end{enumerate}
