\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
    \large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Foundation: Reading Two-Way Tables}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
A \textbf{two-way frequency table} is used to show relationships between two \textbf{categorical} (word-based) variables. It shows how often different categories occur together.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Rows and Columns:} One variable is on the side (rows), and the other is on the top (columns).
    \item \textbf{Joint Frequency:} The numbers in the middle of the table. They show where two categories overlap.
    \item \textbf{Marginal Frequency:} The "Total" column and "Total" row. They show the total for just one category.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{4mm}

% ==========================================================
% VISUAL REPRESENTATION
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Visual Representation: The Pet Survey}

\medskip

Eighth graders were surveyed about whether they have a dog or a cat. 

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
 & \textbf{Has a Dog} & \textbf{No Dog} & \textbf{Total} \\
\hline
\textbf{Has a Cat} & \textcolor{blue}{12} & \textcolor{blue}{18} & \textcolor{red}{30} \\
\hline
\textbf{No Cat} & \textcolor{blue}{40} & \textcolor{blue}{30} & \textcolor{red}{70} \\
\hline
\textbf{Total} & \textcolor{red}{52} & \textcolor{red}{48} & \textcolor{green!50!black}{\textbf{100}} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\medskip
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textcolor{blue}{\textbf{Joint Frequencies:}} $12$ students have BOTH a dog and a cat. $40$ students have a dog but NO cat.
    \item \textcolor{red}{\textbf{Marginal Frequencies:}} $30$ students total have a cat. $52$ students total have a dog.
    \item \textcolor{green!50!black}{\textbf{Grand Total:}} $100$ students were surveyed in total. (Notice that $52+48=100$ and $30+70=100$).
\end{itemize}

\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Relative Frequencies (Percentages)}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
To find a \textbf{relative frequency}, you divide a number in the table by the total to get a decimal or percentage. You can do this out of the grand total, or out of a specific row/column total.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Worked Example 1: Finding Percentages}

\medskip

\textbf{Problem:} Using the pet table, answer the following:
\textbf{a)} What percentage of ALL students have both a dog and a cat?
\textbf{b)} What percentage of students WITH A DOG also have a cat?

\medskip

\textbf{Solution:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Part A (Out of All Students):} Look at the number for "Dog AND Cat" ($12$). Divide by the Grand Total ($100$). 
    \[ \frac{12}{100} = 0.12 = 12\% \]
    \item \textbf{Part B (Out of Dog Owners):} Look at the total number of people who have a dog. The "Total" for the "Dog" column is $52$. Out of those $52$, how many have a cat? It's $12$.
    \[ \frac{12}{52} \approx 0.23 = 23\% \]
    \item \textit{Notice the difference! Reading the question carefully is key.}
\end{itemize}

\end{tcolorbox}
