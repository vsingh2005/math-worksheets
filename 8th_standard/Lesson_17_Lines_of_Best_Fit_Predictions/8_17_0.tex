\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
    \large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Foundation: The Line of Best Fit}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
When data in a scatter plot shows a linear association, we can draw a \textbf{Line of Best Fit} (or trend line). This line helps us model the data with a linear equation ($y=mx+b$) and make predictions.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Drawing the Line:} A good line of best fit should go straight through the "middle" of the data cluster. It should follow the trend, with roughly half the points above the line and half below it. It \textit{does not} need to start at $(0,0)$.
    \item \textbf{Making Predictions:} Once you have the equation of the line, you can plug in an $x$-value to predict a $y$-value, or vice versa.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{4mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Worked Example 1: Predicting Weight from Height}

\medskip

\textbf{Problem:} A line of best fit for a set of data regarding dog height ($x$ in inches) and dog weight ($y$ in pounds) passes through the points $(10, 25)$ and $(20, 55)$. 
\textbf{a)} Write the equation of the line.
\textbf{b)} Predict the weight of a dog that is $15$ inches tall.

\medskip

\textbf{Solution:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Step 1 (Find $m$):} $m = \dfrac{55 - 25}{20 - 10} = \dfrac{30}{10} = 3$. (The dog gains $3$ lbs per inch of height).
    \item \textbf{Step 2 (Find $b$):} Use $y = mx + b$ and point $(10, 25)$.
    \begin{align*}
        25 &= 3(10) + b \\
        25 &= 30 + b \\
        -5 &= b
    \end{align*}
    \item \textbf{Equation:} $y = 3x - 5$.
    \item \textbf{Step 3 (Predict):} The dog is $15$ inches tall ($x = 15$). Plug it in.
    \[ y = 3(15) - 5 \implies y = 45 - 5 = 40 \]
    \item \textbf{Answer:} The predicted weight is $40$ lbs.
\end{itemize}

\end{tcolorbox}
