\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
    \large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Foundation: What is a Function?}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
A \textbf{function} is a specific type of mathematical relationship where every \textbf{input} has exactly one \textbf{output}. 

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Input ($x$):} The independent variable (domain).
    \item \textbf{Output ($y$):} The dependent variable (range).
    \item \textbf{The Rule:} An input can only point to ONE output. If an input points to two different outputs, it is \textbf{not} a function.
    \item \textbf{Vertical Line Test:} When looking at a graph, if you can draw any vertical line that touches the graph more than once, it is \textbf{not} a function.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{4mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Identifying Functions from Tables and Graphs}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
We can identify functions using ordered pairs, tables, or graphs. Look out for repeating $x$-values with different $y$-values!

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Worked Example 1: Tables and the Vertical Line Test}

\medskip

\textbf{Problem A:} Is the table a function?
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{x} & 1 & 2 & 1 & 4 \\
\hline
\textbf{y} & 5 & 6 & 7 & 8 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\medskip

\textbf{Solution A:} \textbf{No.} The input $1$ has two different outputs ($5$ and $7$).

\medskip
\hrule
\medskip

\textbf{Problem B:} Is a circle drawn on a graph a function?

\medskip

\textbf{Solution B:} \textbf{No.} If you draw a vertical line straight down the middle of the circle, it touches the top of the circle and the bottom of the circle. This fails the Vertical Line Test.

\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{4mm}
