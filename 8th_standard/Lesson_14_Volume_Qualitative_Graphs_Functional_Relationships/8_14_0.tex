\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
    \large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Foundation: Qualitative Graphs}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
A \textbf{qualitative graph} represents the relationship between two quantities without using specific numbers on the axes. It focuses on the \textit{behavior} or \textit{trend} of the function.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Increasing:} The graph goes UP from left to right (positive slope).
    \item \textbf{Decreasing:} The graph goes DOWN from left to right (negative slope).
    \item \textbf{Constant:} The graph is a horizontal flat line (zero slope).
    \item \textbf{Linear vs. Non-Linear:} A straight line means a constant rate of change. A curve means the rate of change is accelerating or decelerating.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{4mm}

\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\medskip
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Driving:} Distance is increasing at a steady rate (linear).
    \item \textbf{Traffic:} Time passes, but distance from home doesn't change (constant/horizontal).
    \item \textbf{Highway:} Distance increases faster than before (steeper slope).
    \item \textbf{At Work:} Arrived at destination; distance stays constant.
\end{itemize}

\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Sketching and Interpreting}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
You can sketch a graph from a story, or tell a story from a graph. Pay attention to whether the change is linear (steady) or non-linear (curved).

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Worked Example 1: Filling a Container}

\medskip

\textbf{Problem:} Sketch a qualitative graph showing the water level over time as a cylinder is filled by a hose at a steady rate.

\medskip

\textbf{Solution:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Since the container is a uniform cylinder, the water level will rise at a completely steady, unchanging rate.
    \item The graph will be a straight line starting from zero and going up.
    \item \textbf{What if it was a cone?} If the cone is narrow at the bottom and wide at the top, it fills fast at first, then slower. The graph would be a curve getting less steep over time!
\end{itemize}

\end{tcolorbox}
