\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=black!10]
    \large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Statistical Questions and Distribution Characteristics (Foundation)}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
1. \textbf{Statistical Question:} A question that anticipates variability in the data (i.e., there is not just one single answer). \\
- \textit{Non-statistical:} ``How old am I?'' (only 1 answer) \\
- \textit{Statistical:} ``How old are the students in my class?'' (answers will vary) \\
2. \textbf{Data Distribution:} Summarized by its: \\
- \textbf{Center:} The average or middle value (mean, median). \\
- \textbf{Spread:} How spread out the data is (range, interquartile range). \\
- \textbf{Shape:} Symmetric (balanced sides), skewed left (tail on left), or skewed right (tail on right).

\vspace{4mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]
\textbf{Worked Example:}

\medskip
\noindent
Identify if ``How many books did each student read last month?'' is statistical or non-statistical, and predict its shape if most read 1-2 books but one student read 20.\\
\textbf{Solution:}
- It is a \textbf{statistical question} because we expect the number of books read by different students to vary.
- Since most values are small (1-2) and there is a single very high value (20), the distribution will be \textbf{skewed right}.
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{4mm}
