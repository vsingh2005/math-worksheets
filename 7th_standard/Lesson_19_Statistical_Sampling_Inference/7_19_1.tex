% PART A - MAKING INFERENCES
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Part A: Proportional Inferences}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
You can use the results of a random sample to make an \textbf{inference} (a smart prediction) about the entire population using a proportion.
\[ \dfrac{\text{Number in Sample with Trait}}{\text{Total Sample Size}} = \dfrac{\text{Predicted Number in Population}}{\text{Total Population Size}} \]

\vspace{0.5cm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3.0cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A middle school has $800$ students. You randomly sample $50$ students and find that $12$ of them ride the bus to school. Based on this sample, predict how many students in the entire school ride the bus.

\item A factory produces $5,000$ lightbulbs every day. The quality control manager randomly tests $200$ lightbulbs and finds that $3$ of them are defective. Predict the total number of defective lightbulbs produced in one day.

\end{enumerate}
