\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Instruction: Populations vs. Samples}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
\textbf{Key Terms:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Population:} The entire group of people or objects you want to know about.
    \item \textbf{Sample:} A smaller part of the population that is actually surveyed or measured.
    \item \textbf{Random Sample:} A sample where every member of the population has an equal chance of being selected. This is the \textit{best} kind of sample because it is \textbf{unbiased}.
    \item \textbf{Biased Sample:} A sample that does not fairly represent the population.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{2mm}
\noindent
\textbf{Example:} You want to know the favorite food of all students in your school.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Population:} Every student in the school.
    \item \textit{Random Sample:} Pulling 50 names out of a hat containing all students' names.
    \item \textit{Biased Sample:} Asking only the 50 students in the cafeteria line on Pizza Day.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{5mm}
