% SECTION 1: THE RULES OF SIGNED NUMBERS
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
    \large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Multiplying \& Dividing Signed Rational Numbers}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
When multiplying or dividing rational numbers (integers, fractions, and decimals), we follow the exact same rules for signs that we use for integers.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Same Signs} yield a \textbf{positive} result:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $\text{Positive} \times \text{Positive} = \text{Positive}$ \quad or \quad $(+) \cdot (+) = (+)$
        \item $\text{Negative} \times \text{Negative} = \text{Positive}$ \quad or \quad $(-) \cdot (-) = (+)$
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Different Signs} yield a \textbf{negative} result:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $\text{Positive} \times \text{Negative} = \text{Negative}$ \quad or \quad $(+) \cdot (-) = (-)$
        \item $\text{Negative} \times \text{Positive} = \text{Negative}$ \quad or \quad $(-) \cdot (+) = (-)$
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\vspace{4mm}

% ==========================================================
% VISUAL REPRESENTATION / WORKED EXAMPLES
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Examples with Fractions and Decimals}

\medskip

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\Alph*.}]
    \item \textbf{Multiplying Decimals:} $(-2.5) \times (-0.4)$
    \begin{itemize}
        \item First, determine the sign: both signs are negative (same), so the answer is \textbf{positive}.
        \item Multiply the absolute values: $2.5 \times 0.4 = 1.0$
        \item Answer: $1$ or $1.0$
    \end{itemize}
    
    \medskip
    
    \item \textbf{Dividing Fractions:} $\left(-\dfrac{3}{4}\right) \div \left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)$
    \begin{itemize}
        \item First, determine the sign: one negative and one positive (different), so the answer is \textbf{negative}.
        \item Use "Keep-Change-Flip" to divide:
        \[
        \frac{3}{4} \div \frac{2}{3} = \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{3}{2} = \frac{9}{8}
        \]
        \item Answer: $-\dfrac{9}{8}$ or $-1\dfrac{1}{8}$
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Interpreting Rational Operations in Real Life}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Real-world scenarios often use negative rational numbers to represent decreases, drops in elevation, debts, or temperature changes. 

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Worked Example: Temperature Drop}

\medskip

\textbf{Problem:} Over a $4.5$-hour period, the temperature in Chicago dropped at a constant rate of $1.2^\circ\text{F}$ per hour. What was the total change in temperature?

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Solution:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A drop of $1.2^\circ\text{F}$ per hour can be represented as $-1.2$.
    \item The total time is $4.5$ hours.
    \item Set up the multiplication:
    \[
    \text{Total Change} = \text{Rate} \times \text{Time} = (-1.2) \times 4.5
    \]
    \item Multiply the absolute values: $1.2 \times 4.5 = 5.4$
    \item Since the signs are different, the product is negative: $-5.4$.
\end{itemize}

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Answer:} The temperature changed by $-5.4^\circ\text{F}$ (a total drop of $5.4^\circ\text{F}$).

\end{tcolorbox}
