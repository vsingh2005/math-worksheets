\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Instruction: Solving and Graphing Inequalities}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
Solving a two-step inequality is just like solving a two-step equation, with one \textbf{MAJOR EXCEPTION}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{THE FLIP RULE:} If you multiply or divide both sides by a \textbf{negative} number, you MUST flip the inequality symbol! (e.g. $<$ becomes $>$)
\end{itemize}

\vspace{2mm}
\noindent
\textbf{Graphing Inequalities on a Number Line:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Open Circle ($\circ$):} Use for $<$ or $>$ (does not include the number).
    \item \textbf{Closed Circle ($\bullet$):} Use for $\le$ or $\ge$ (includes the number).
    \item \textbf{Arrow:} Shade the number line in the direction of the values that make the inequality true.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{3mm}
\noindent
\textbf{Example:} $-2x + 5 \ge 11$
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Step 1:} Subtract 5 from both sides. $\rightarrow -2x \ge 6$
    \item \textbf{Step 2:} Divide by -2 and \textbf{FLIP THE SYMBOL}. $\rightarrow x \le -3$
\end{itemize}

\vspace{5mm}
