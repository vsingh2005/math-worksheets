\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Instruction: Breaking Down Composite Figures}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
A \textbf{composite figure} is a shape made up of two or more simpler shapes (like rectangles, triangles, or semicircles).

\vspace{2mm}
\noindent
\textbf{How to find the Area:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Decompose:} Break the complex figure down into simpler shapes that you know the formulas for.
    \item \textbf{Calculate:} Find the area of each simpler shape separately.
    \item \textbf{Combine:} \textit{Add} the areas together if the shapes are joined. \textit{Subtract} the area if a shape is a "hole" cut out of the main figure.
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{2mm}
\noindent
\textbf{Formulas to Remember:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Rectangle: $A = b \times h$
    \item Triangle: $A = \dfrac{1}{2}bh$
    \item Circle: $A = \pi r^2$ (Use half for a semicircle)
\end{itemize}
