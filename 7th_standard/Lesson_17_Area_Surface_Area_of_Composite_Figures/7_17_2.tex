\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Part A: Surface Area of Rectangular Prisms}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
The \textbf{surface area} is the total area of all the faces (sides, top, and bottom) of a 3D object added together. For a rectangular prism, there are 6 faces (3 pairs of matching rectangles).

\vspace{0.5cm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3.0cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A rectangular box has a length of $5\text{ cm}$, a width of $3\text{ cm}$, and a height of $4\text{ cm}$. Find the area of all six faces and add them up to find the total surface area.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Front/Back Faces ($5 \times 4$): \blanklong
    \item Top/Bottom Faces ($5 \times 3$): \blanklong
    \item Left/Right Faces ($3 \times 4$): \blanklong
    \item Total Surface Area: \blanklong
\end{itemize}

\item Find the total surface area of a cube with a side length of $6\text{ inches}$.

\end{enumerate}

% ==========================================================
% PART B - COMPOSITE SURFACE AREA
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Part B: Surface Area of Composite 3D Figures}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
When two 3D objects are stuck together, the faces where they touch are \textit{hidden} and should NOT be counted in the surface area.

\vspace{0.5cm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3.0cm, leftmargin=0.6cm, resume]

\item Two identical cubes, each with a side length of $2\text{ cm}$, are glued together face-to-face to form a rectangular prism.
\begin{itemize}
    \item What is the surface area of just ONE of the cubes before gluing? \blank
    \item How many square faces are hidden once they are glued together? \blank
    \item What is the total surface area of the new composite rectangular prism? \blank
\end{itemize}

\item A square pyramid sits perfectly on top of a cube. Both have a base side length of $4\text{ cm}$. If you are painting the entire outside of this new composite figure, do you paint the top of the cube? Do you paint the bottom of the pyramid? Explain which faces you actually need to calculate the area for.

\end{enumerate}
