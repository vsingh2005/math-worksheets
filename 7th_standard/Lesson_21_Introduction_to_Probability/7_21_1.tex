% ==========================================================
% PART A - THEORETICAL VS EXPERIMENTAL
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Part A: Theoretical vs. Experimental}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
\textbf{Theoretical Probability:} What \textit{should} happen mathematically. \\
\textbf{Experimental Probability:} What \textit{actually} happened when you ran a real experiment.

\vspace{0.5cm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3.0cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A spinner is divided into $4$ equal colored sections: Red, Blue, Green, and Yellow.
\begin{itemize}
    \item What is the \textbf{theoretical} probability of landing on Red? \blank
    \item You spin the spinner $20$ times. It lands on Red $8$ times. What is the \textbf{experimental} probability of landing on Red? \blank
\end{itemize}

\item If you spin the spinner $1,000$ times instead of $20$ times, what do you expect will happen to the experimental probability? Will it get closer to or farther from the theoretical probability? Explain.

\end{enumerate}

\vspace{5cm}

% ==========================================================
% PART B - PREDICTING OUTCOMES
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Part B: Making Predictions}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Use probability to predict how many times an event will occur.

\vspace{0.5cm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3.0cm, leftmargin=0.6cm, resume]

\item The probability of a basketball player making a free throw is $0.75$ (or $\frac{3}{4}$). If the player shoots $60$ free throws in practice, how many do you predict they will make?

\item You roll a 6-sided die $300$ times. Predict how many times you will roll a $5$. (Hint: Multiply the theoretical probability by the number of trials).

\end{enumerate}
