\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Part A: Prisms with Other Bases}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
You can find the volume of a prism with \textit{any} shape base as long as you know the area of that base. $V = B \times h$.

\vspace{0.5cm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3.0cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A trapezoidal prism has a base area of $40\text{ cm}^2$. The prism is $15\text{ cm}$ long. What is the volume of the prism?

\item A hexagonal prism has a volume of $300\text{ in}^3$. If the height of the prism is $12\text{ in}$, what is the area of the hexagonal base?

\item A prism has a base shaped like a heart. The area of the heart base is $25\text{ square units}$. The height of the prism is $8\text{ units}$. What is the volume?

\end{enumerate}

\vspace{4cm}
