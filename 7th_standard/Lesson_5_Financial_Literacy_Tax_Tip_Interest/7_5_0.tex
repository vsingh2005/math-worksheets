% SECTION 1: TAX, TIP, AND MARKUPS
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
    \large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Percent Additions: Tax, Tip, \& Markup}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
In financial math, some percents are \textbf{added} to the original cost. 
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sales Tax:} An extra charge added by the government on purchases.
    \item \textbf{Tip (Gratuity):} Extra money given to a service worker (like a waiter or driver) for their service.
    \item \textbf{Markup:} The amount a store increases the wholesale price of an item to make a profit.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{2mm}
\noindent
There are \textbf{two methods} to solve percent addition problems:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Two-Step Method:} Find the percent of the original price first, then add it to the original price.
    \item \textbf{One-Step Method:} Add the percent to $100\%$, convert to a decimal, and multiply. (For example, a $7\%$ tax means you pay $107\%$ of the price, or $1.07 \times \text{Price}$).
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{4mm}

% ==========================================================
% VISUAL REPRESENTATION / WORKED EXAMPLE 1
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Example: Dining Out}

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Problem:} A meal at a restaurant costs $\$32.00$. You want to leave a $15\%$ tip. What is the total cost of the meal?

\medskip

\begin{multicols}{2}
\noindent
\textbf{Method 1: Two-Step}
\begin{align*}
    \text{Tip} &= 15\% \text{ of } \$32.00 \\
    &= 0.15 \times 32.00 \\
    &= \$4.80 \\[1mm]
    \text{Total} &= \text{Original} + \text{Tip} \\
    &= 32.00 + 4.80 \\
    &= \$36.80
\end{align*}

\columnbreak

\noindent
\textbf{Method 2: One-Step}
\begin{align*}
    \text{Total Percent} &= 100\% + 15\% \\
    &= 115\% \\
    &= 1.15 \text{ (as a decimal)} \\[1mm]
    \text{Total Cost} &= 1.15 \times 32.00 \\
    &= \$36.80
\end{align*}
\end{multicols}

\end{tcolorbox}

% ==========================================================
% SECTION 2: DISCOUNTS AND MARKDOWNS
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Percent Subtractions: Discounts \& Markdowns}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
Some percents are \textbf{subtracted} from the original cost.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Discount / Markdown:} A reduction in the original price of an item (usually during a sale).
\end{itemize}

\vspace{2mm}
\noindent
Just like percent additions, you can use two methods:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Two-Step Method:} Find the discount amount first, then subtract it from the original price.
    \item \textbf{One-Step Method:} Subtract the discount percent from $100\%$, convert to a decimal, and multiply. (For example, a $20\%$ discount means you pay $80\%$ of the original price, or $0.80 \times \text{Price}$).
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{4mm}

% ==========================================================
% WORKED EXAMPLE 2
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Example: Buying a Skateboard}

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Problem:} A skateboard is originally priced at $\$80.00$. It is on sale for $25\%$ off. What is the sale price?

\medskip

\begin{multicols}{2}
\noindent
\textbf{Method 1: Two-Step}
\begin{align*}
    \text{Discount} &= 25\% \text{ of } \$80.00 \\
    &= 0.25 \times 80.00 \\
    &= \$20.00 \\[1mm]
    \text{Sale Price} &= \text{Original} - \text{Discount} \\
    &= 80.00 - 20.00 \\
    &= \$60.00
\end{align*}

\columnbreak

\noindent
\textbf{Method 2: One-Step}
\begin{align*}
    \text{Percent Paid} &= 100\% - 25\% \\
    &= 75\% \\
    &= 0.75 \text{ (as a decimal)} \\[1mm]
    \text{Sale Price} &= 0.75 \times 80.00 \\
    &= \$60.00
\end{align*}
\end{multicols}

\end{tcolorbox}

% ==========================================================
% SECTION 3: SIMPLE INTEREST
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Simple Interest: $I = Prt$}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
When you deposit money in a bank, the bank pays you for keeping your money there. When you borrow money, you must pay back the original amount plus an extra fee. This extra money is called \textbf{interest}.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{center}
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black, width=12cm, arc=3mm, align=center]
    \Large\textbf{Simple Interest Formula}
    \vspace{2mm}
    \[ \mathbf{I = Prt} \]
    \normalsize
    \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.5cm, labelsep=0.3cm]
        \item[\textbf{I}] = Interest earned or owed (in dollars)
        \item[\textbf{P}] = Principal (the starting amount of money)
        \item[\textbf{r}] = Annual interest rate (written as a \textbf{decimal})
        \item[\textbf{t}] = Time (written in \textbf{years})
    \end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}
\end{center}

\vspace{3mm}

% ==========================================================
% WORKED EXAMPLE 3
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Example: Savings Account Interest}

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Problem:} You deposit $\$500$ into a savings account that earns $3\%$ simple interest annually. How much interest will you earn after $4$ years? What will your total balance be?

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Step 1: Identify your variables.}
\[ P = \$500, \quad r = 3\% = 0.03, \quad t = 4 \text{ years} \]

\noindent
\textbf{Step 2: Use the formula to find the interest ($I$).}
\[ I = P \cdot r \cdot t \]
\[ I = 500 \cdot 0.03 \cdot 4 \]
\[ I = 15 \cdot 4 = \$60 \]

\noindent
\textbf{Step 3: Calculate the total balance.}
\[ \text{Total Balance} = \text{Principal} + \text{Interest} = 500 + 60 = \$560 \]

\end{tcolorbox}
