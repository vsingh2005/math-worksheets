% PART A - SIMULATION
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Part A: Designing a Simulation}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
Sometimes an experiment is too difficult to run in real life, so we use a \textbf{simulation} (like a spinner, dice, or coin) that has the \textit{exact same probability}.

\vspace{0.5cm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3.0cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A cereal brand is running a promotion where $1$ out of every $6$ boxes contains a special prize. You want to simulate buying boxes of cereal until you win the prize. What tool (coin, spinner, dice, deck of cards) could you use for this simulation, and what rule would you set for "Winning"?

\item A basketball player makes exactly $50\%$ of his shots. You want to simulate him taking $10$ shots. What tool would be the best to use, and what would your rules be?

\end{enumerate}

\vspace{4.5cm}

% ==========================================================
% PART B - ANALYZING SIMULATIONS
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Part B: Evaluating the Results}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Think critically about simulation data.

\vspace{0.5cm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3.0cm, leftmargin=0.6cm, resume]

\item A student flips a coin $3$ times to simulate a family having $3$ children ($H = \text{Boy}, T = \text{Girl}$). They do this $20$ times. Out of the $20$ trials, $8$ trials resulted in exactly $2$ boys. Based on this simulation, what is the experimental probability of a family having exactly $2$ boys out of $3$ children? 

\end{enumerate}
