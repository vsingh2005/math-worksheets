\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
\textbf{Key Angle Types:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Complementary Angles:} Two angles whose sum is exactly $\mathbf{90^\circ}$ (a right angle).
    \item \textbf{Supplementary Angles:} Two angles whose sum is exactly $\mathbf{180^\circ}$ (a straight line).
    \item \textbf{Vertical Angles:} Angles opposite each other when two lines cross. Vertical angles are always \textbf{equal} to each other.
    \item \textbf{Adjacent Angles:} Angles that are right next to each other, sharing a common side and vertex.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{2mm}
\noindent
\textbf{Using Algebra:} If you know two angles are supplementary, you can write: $\text{Angle 1} + \text{Angle 2} = 180$.

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
Use the definitions of angle relationships to find the missing angle measure.

\vspace{0.5cm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=4.5cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item Angles A and B are \textbf{complementary}. If Angle A is $35^\circ$, what is the measure of Angle B?

\item Angles C and D are \textbf{supplementary}. If Angle C is $115^\circ$, what is the measure of Angle D?

\item Angles X and Y are \textbf{vertical} angles. If Angle X is $42^\circ$, what is the measure of Angle Y?

\item Two adjacent angles form a right angle ($90^\circ$). One angle measures $22^\circ$. Write an equation and solve for the other angle, $x$.

\end{enumerate}
