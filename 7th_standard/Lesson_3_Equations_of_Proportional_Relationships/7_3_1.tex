\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Part A: Equations of Proportional Relationships}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
A proportional relationship can be represented by an equation of the form $y = kx$, where $k$ is the constant of proportionality (also known as the unit rate).

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
\textbf{Key Points on the Graph:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The origin $(0, 0)$ represents a starting point where there is zero of one quantity and zero of the other. For example, if $y$ is the distance traveled and $x$ is the time, $(0,0)$ means at 0 hours, 0 miles are traveled.
    \item The point $(1, k)$ represents the unit rate. If $k$ is the cost per pound, then $(1, k)$ means 1 pound costs $k$ dollars. If $k$ is the speed in miles per hour, then $(1, k)$ means in 1 hour, $k$ miles are traveled.
\end{itemize}
