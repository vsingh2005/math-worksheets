\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Part C: The Ambiguous Case}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Sometimes giving two sides and an angle doesn't create a unique triangle. Think critically to solve this problem.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3.0cm, leftmargin=0.6cm, resume]

\item A student is told to draw a triangle with sides measuring $5\text{ cm}$ and $8\text{ cm}$. They are also told that one of the angles in the triangle must be exactly $30^\circ$. Is there only \textit{one} specific triangle that can be drawn with these three pieces of information, or could the student draw more than one different-looking triangle that fits these rules? Explain your reasoning. (Hint: Think about where the angle could be placed).

\end{enumerate}
