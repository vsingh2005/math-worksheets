% ==========================================================
% SECTION 1: WHAT IS A UNIT RATE?
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
    \large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{What is a Unit Rate with Fractions?}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
A \textbf{unit rate} is a rate where the second quantity is exactly $1$ unit. For example, "miles per hour" means miles for every $1$ hour. 

\begin{itemize}
    \item When dealing with fractions, finding a unit rate involves \textbf{dividing a fraction by a fraction}.
    \item A \textbf{complex fraction} is a fraction where the numerator, the denominator, or both contain a fraction, such as $\dfrac{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{4}}$.
    \item To compute the unit rate, divide the first quantity by the second quantity.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{4mm}

% ==========================================================
% VISUAL REPRESENTATION
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Example: Walking Speed}

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Problem:} Sarah walks $\dfrac{1}{2}$ mile in $\dfrac{1}{4}$ hour. What is her speed in miles per hour?

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Set up the ratio:} We want \textit{miles per hour}, which means miles divided by hours:
\[
\frac{\text{Miles}}{\text{Hours}} = \frac{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{4}}
\]

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Solve by dividing fractions:} "Keep-Change-Flip"
\[
\frac{1}{2} \div \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{4}{1} = \frac{4}{2} = 2
\]

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Answer:} Sarah walks $2$ miles per hour.

\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{4mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Comparing Unit Rates}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
We can use unit rates to find the "better buy" or to determine who is faster.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Worked Example: Who is Faster?}

\medskip

\textbf{Problem:} 
\begin{itemize}
    \item Runner A completes $\dfrac{3}{4}$ mile in $\dfrac{1}{6}$ of an hour.
    \item Runner B completes $\dfrac{5}{8}$ mile in $\dfrac{1}{8}$ of an hour.
\end{itemize}
Who runs faster (in miles per hour)?

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Solution:}

\noindent
\textbf{Runner A:} 
\[ \frac{3}{4} \div \frac{1}{6} = \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{6}{1} = \frac{18}{4} = 4.5 \text{ miles per hour} \]

\noindent
\textbf{Runner B:} 
\[ \frac{5}{8} \div \frac{1}{8} = \frac{5}{8} \times \frac{8}{1} = \frac{40}{8} = 5 \text{ miles per hour} \]

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Answer:} Runner B is faster ($5$ mph vs $4.5$ mph).

\end{tcolorbox}
