\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Instruction: Cross Sections of 3D Figures}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
When you slice a 3D figure, the newly exposed 2D flat surface is called a \textbf{cross section}. The shape of the cross section depends on the 3D figure and how you slice it.

\vspace{2mm}
\noindent
\textbf{Important Slices:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Parallel to the Base (Horizontal):} The slice goes straight across, side to side.
    \item \textbf{Perpendicular to the Base (Vertical):} The slice goes straight down, from top to bottom.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{2mm}
\noindent
\textbf{Examples for a Rectangular Prism:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Parallel slice:} The cross section is a rectangle identical to the base.
    \item \textit{Perpendicular slice:} The cross section is a rectangle identical to the side face.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{5mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Practice: Name the Cross Section}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
Identify the 2D shape of the cross section formed by the given slice. Write the name of the shape.

\vspace{0.5cm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=4.5cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A \textbf{Cube} is sliced \textit{parallel} to its base. What shape is the cross section?

\item A \textbf{Square Pyramid} is sliced \textit{parallel} to its base. What shape is the cross section?

\item A \textbf{Square Pyramid} is sliced \textit{perpendicular} (straight down from the very top point) to its base. What shape is the cross section?

\item A \textbf{Cylinder} is sliced \textit{parallel} to its circular base. What shape is the cross section?

\item A \textbf{Cylinder} is sliced \textit{perpendicular} to its base (straight down). What shape is the cross section?

\end{enumerate}
