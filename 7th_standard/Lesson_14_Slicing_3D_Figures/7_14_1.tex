% PART A - VISUALIZING SLICES
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Part A: Visualizing the Cross Section}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
For each scenario, describe the 2D shape that is created when the object is sliced. 

\vspace{0.5cm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3.0cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A rectangular prism (like a shoebox) has a base of $8\text{ in}$ by $5\text{ in}$ and a height of $12\text{ in}$. It is sliced parallel to the base. Describe the shape and dimensions of the cross section.

\item The same rectangular prism ($8\text{ in}$ by $5\text{ in}$ base, $12\text{ in}$ height) is sliced perpendicular to the base, cutting exactly through the $5\text{ in}$ width. Describe the shape and dimensions of the cross section.

\item A cone is sliced parallel to its base. Describe the shape of the cross section. Is it the same size as the base? Why or why not?

\end{enumerate}

\vspace{4.5cm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Part B: Matching Shapes}}

\noindent
Match the 3D figure and the slice direction to the resulting 2D cross section shape. Draw a line to connect them.

\vspace{0.5cm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3.0cm, leftmargin=0.6cm, resume]

\item Cylinder sliced parallel to the base \hfill \textbf{A.} Rectangle

\item Cube sliced perpendicular to the base \hfill \textbf{B.} Circle

\item Square Pyramid sliced perpendicular to the base \hfill \textbf{C.} Square

\item Cone sliced perpendicular through the top point \hfill \textbf{D.} Triangle

\end{enumerate}
