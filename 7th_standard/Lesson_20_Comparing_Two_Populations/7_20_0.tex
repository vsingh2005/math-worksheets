\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Instruction: Center and Spread}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
When comparing two different populations (e.g., the heights of 7th graders vs. 8th graders), we look at two main things:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Measures of Center (Mean or Median):} Tells you what is "typical" or "average." A higher mean means that group is generally larger/taller/faster overall.
    \item \textbf{Measures of Variability/Spread (Range or Mean Absolute Deviation):} Tells you how spread out the data is. A larger spread means the data is less predictable and more varied. A smaller spread means the data is tightly clustered and more consistent.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
Read the data summaries and answer the questions.

\vspace{0.5cm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=4.5cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A teacher compared the test scores of her morning class and her afternoon class.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Morning Class:} Mean = $82\%$, Range = $25\%$
    \item \textbf{Afternoon Class:} Mean = $74\%$, Range = $10\%$
\end{itemize}
\textbf{A.} Which class did better overall on average? \blank \\
\textbf{B.} Which class had more consistent (less spread out) scores? \blank

\item Two different brands of batteries were tested to see how many hours they last.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Brand X:} Median = $45\text{ hours}$, Mean Absolute Deviation = $2\text{ hours}$
    \item \textbf{Brand Y:} Median = $45\text{ hours}$, Mean Absolute Deviation = $12\text{ hours}$
\end{itemize}
If you need a battery that you are \textit{very certain} will last close to $45$ hours, which brand should you buy? Why?

\end{enumerate}
