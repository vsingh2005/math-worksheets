\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
    \large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Percent Increase and Percent Decrease}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
A \textbf{percent of change} measures how much a quantity increases or decreases relative to its original amount. We express this change as a percentage of the original value.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Percent Increase} occurs when the new value is \textit{greater} than the original value (e.g., markups, tax, tips).
    \item \textbf{Percent Decrease} occurs when the new value is \textit{less} than the original value (e.g., discounts, sales, markdowns).
    \item To find the percent of change, use the following formula:
\end{itemize}

\[
\text{Percent of Change} = \frac{\text{Amount of Change}}{\text{Original Amount}} \times 100\%
\]

\noindent
where $\text{Amount of Change} = |\text{New Value} - \text{Original Value}|$.

\vspace{4mm}

% ==========================================================
% VISUAL REPRESENTATION / WORKED EXAMPLE
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Example: Discount on a Jacket}

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Problem:} A winter coat originally costs $\$80$. During a spring clearance sale, the price is reduced to $\$60$. What is the percent decrease?

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Step 1: Find the amount of change (decrease).}
\[
\text{Amount of Change} = \$80 - \$60 = \$20
\]

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Step 2: Set up the ratio using the original amount.}
\[
\text{Percent Decrease} = \frac{\text{Amount of Change}}{\text{Original Amount}} = \frac{20}{80}
\]

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Step 3: Convert the fraction to a percentage.}
\[
\frac{20}{80} = \frac{1}{4} = 0.25 = 25\%
\]

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Answer:} The price of the jacket decreased by $25\%$.

\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{4mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Percent Error}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
\textbf{Percent error} is a calculation used to compare an estimated, measured, or predicted value to the actual, exact value. It tells us how far off an estimate is relative to the size of the actual value.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Because an error represents a distance from the correct value, percent error is \textbf{always expressed as a positive number} (we use absolute value).
\end{itemize}

\[
\text{Percent Error} = \frac{|\text{Estimated Value} - \text{Actual Value}|}{\text{Actual Value}} \times 100\%
\]

\vspace{4mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Worked Example: Party Attendance}

\medskip

\textbf{Problem:} 
Marcus estimates that $50$ people will attend the school talent show. The actual attendance is $62$ people. What is the percent error of Marcus's estimate? (Round to the nearest tenth of a percent).

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Solution:}

\noindent
\textbf{Step 1: Find the absolute difference (error).}
\[ |\text{Estimated} - \text{Actual}| = |50 - 62| = |-12| = 12 \]

\noindent
\textbf{Step 2: Divide by the actual value.}
\[ \text{Percent Error} = \frac{12}{62} \approx 0.1935 \]

\noindent
\textbf{Step 3: Convert to a percent.}
\[ 0.1935 \times 100\% \approx 19.4\% \]

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Answer:} Marcus's estimate has a $19.4\%$ error.

\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{4mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Multi-Step Percent Problems}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Real-world scenarios often require combining multiple percent concepts, such as applying a discount first, then calculating sales tax, tips, or commissions on the final amounts.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Worked Example: Discount then Tax}

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Problem:} A tablet is priced at $\$200$. It is on sale for $15\%$ off. If the sales tax is $8\%$, how much will you pay for the tablet in total?

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Step 1: Find the sale price (markdown).}
\[ \text{Discount} = 15\% \text{ of } 200 = 0.15 \times 200 = \$30 \]
\[ \text{Sale Price} = \$200 - \$30 = \$170 \]

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Step 2: Calculate sales tax on the sale price.}
\[ \text{Sales Tax} = 8\% \text{ of } 170 = 0.08 \times 170 = \$13.60 \]

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Step 3: Find the total cost.}
\[ \text{Total Cost} = \$170 + \$13.60 = \$183.60 \]

\end{tcolorbox}
