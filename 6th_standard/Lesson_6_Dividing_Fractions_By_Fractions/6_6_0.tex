% ==========================================================
% SECTION 1: FOUNDATION — RECIPROCALS AND FRACTION DIVISION
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=black!10]
    \large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Reciprocals and Fraction Division (Foundation)}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
\textbf{What is a Reciprocal?} The reciprocal of a fraction $\dfrac{a}{b}$ is $\dfrac{b}{a}$ (flip the numerator and denominator). The product of any number and its reciprocal is always 1.
$$\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{4}{3} = \frac{12}{12} = 1 \qquad \qquad 5 \times \frac{1}{5} = 1$$

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
\textbf{How to Divide Fractions:} To divide a fraction by another fraction, multiply the first fraction by the \textbf{reciprocal} of the second fraction. This is sometimes called \textit{Keep, Change, Flip (KCF)}.
$$\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \times \frac{d}{c} = \frac{ad}{bc}$$

\noindent
\textbf{Example:}
$$\frac{2}{3} \div \frac{4}{5} = \frac{2}{3} \times \frac{5}{4} = \frac{10}{12} = \frac{5}{6}$$

\vspace{4mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=4mm, bottom=4mm]
\textbf{Pro-Tip: Handling Whole Numbers \& Mixed Numbers}

\noindent
Before you can find a reciprocal or use Keep-Change-Flip, you must rewrite whole numbers and mixed numbers as \textbf{improper fractions}!
\vspace{1cm}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Whole Numbers:} Put the number over 1. \quad $\rightarrow \quad 6 = \dfrac{6}{1}$
    \vspace{1cm}
    \item \textbf{Mixed Numbers:} Use the "Mad" method (Multiply, Add, Denominator). 
    $$2\dfrac{1}{3} \rightarrow \dfrac{(3 \times 2) + 1}{3} = \dfrac{7}{3}$$
    \vspace{0.5cm}
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{4mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]
\textbf{Worked Example:}

\medskip
\noindent
A pitcher contains $\dfrac{3}{4}$ liters of water. If each glass holds $\dfrac{1}{8}$ liter, how many glasses can be filled?\\[4pt]

\vspace{0.8cm}
\textbf{Step 1 — Identify the division:} $\dfrac{3}{4} \div \dfrac{1}{8}$\\[6pt]

\vspace{0.4cm}

\textbf{Step 2 — Find the reciprocal of the divisor:} reciprocal of $\dfrac{1}{8}$ is $\dfrac{8}{1}$\\[6pt]

\vspace{0.4cm}

\textbf{Step 3 — Multiply:} $\dfrac{3}{4} \times \dfrac{8}{1} = \dfrac{24}{4} = \mathbf{6}$ glasses.
\vspace{1cm}
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=black!10]
    \large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Working Backwards: Variable Equations}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}
\noindent
Because division is the inverse operation of multiplication, we can use families of facts to find missing values:
$$\text{If } \ \frac{2}{3} \times x = \frac{4}{9} \quad \longrightarrow \quad \text{Solve by dividing: } \ x = \frac{4}{9} \div \frac{2}{3}$$

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]
\textbf{Special Case: Variable as the Divisor}

\medskip
\textbf{Problem:} Solve for $b$: \ $\dfrac{3}{8} \div b = \dfrac{3}{4}$

\medskip
\noindent
\textbf{Think:} $10 \div ? = 2$ means $10 \div 2 = ?$. We follow the exact same structure with fractions!
$$b = \frac{3}{8} \div \frac{3}{4} \quad \rightarrow \quad b = \frac{3}{8} \times \frac{4}{3} = \frac{12}{24} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{2}}$$
\end{tcolorbox}
