% ==========================================================
% PART A — REAL-WORLD APPLICATIONS
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=black!10]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Real-World Area Applications}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Solve. Show all work and include units.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1.5cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A backyard is shaped like a trapezoid with parallel sides of 20 meters and 30 meters, and a height of 15 meters. Find the total area of the backyard.

\vspace{2cm}

\item If a bag of grass seed covers 50 square meters, how many bags do you need to seed the backyard from Problem 1?

\vspace{2cm}

\item An artist is painting a triangular wooden sign with a base of 3.5 feet and a height of 2 feet. If one can of paint covers 1.5 square feet, will one can be enough? Justify your answer.

\vspace{2cm}

\end{enumerate}

\newpage

% ==========================================================
% PART B — DECOMPOSING POLYGONS
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=black!10]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Decomposing Composite Figures}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Break each figure into simpler shapes, then find the total area.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2.3cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item An L-shaped floor has a rectangular section of 10 ft $\times$ 6 ft and a triangular section with base 6 ft and height 4 ft. Find the total floor area.

\vspace{2.2cm}

\item A composite figure consists of a parallelogram with base 12 cm and height 5 cm, plus a triangle with base 12 cm and height 8 cm sitting on top. Find the total area.

\vspace{2.2cm}

\item A trapezoidal wall has parallel bases of 9 feet and 15 feet, and a height of 6 feet. A rectangular window (2 ft $\times$ 3 ft) is cut from the wall. What is the remaining painted area?

\vspace{2.5cm}

\end{enumerate}

\newpage

% ==========================================================
% PART C — REASONING AND COMPARISONS
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=black!10]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Reasoning About Area}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Think carefully and explain your reasoning.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2.3cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item Two triangles each have an area of 36 cm$^2$. Triangle A has a base of 12 cm. Triangle B has a base of 9 cm. Find the height of each triangle.

\vspace{2.2cm}

\item A parallelogram and a rectangle have the same base (8 m) and the same height (5 m). Compare their areas. What do you notice?

\vspace{2.2cm}

\item A kite has diagonals of 14 in and 8 in. A rhombus has diagonals of 10 in and 11 in. Which shape has the greater area? By how much?

\vspace{2.5cm}

\end{enumerate}

\newpage

% ==========================================================
% PART D — CHALLENGE PROBLEMS
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=black!10]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Challenge Problems}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Reason through complex, multi-step area scenarios.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2.4cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A triangular plot of land has an area of 150 square meters. Its height is three times its base. Write and solve an equation to find the base and height.

\vspace{2.3cm}

\item A homeowner wants to tile an accent wall shaped like a trapezoid with bases of 10 feet and 14 feet, and a height of 8 feet. Tiles cost \$18.50 per square foot. How much will the tiles cost in total? Round to the nearest cent.

\vspace{2.3cm}

\item A park has a triangular section with base 50 meters and height 30 meters, and a trapezoidal section with bases of 40 meters and 60 meters, and a height of 20 meters. Find the total area of the park. If fencing costs \$5 per meter and the perimeter of the triangular section is 120 meters, what is the cost to fence only that section?

\vspace{2.5cm}

\end{enumerate}
