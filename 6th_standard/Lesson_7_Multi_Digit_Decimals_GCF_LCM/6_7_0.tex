\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=black!10]
    \large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Multi-Digit Decimals and GCF/LCM (Foundation)}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
1. \textbf{Decimal Operations:} Line up decimals for addition and subtraction. For multiplication, count total decimal places. For division, multiply divisor by a power of 10 to make it a whole number. \\ \\
2. \textbf{GCF (Greatest Common Factor):} The largest factor shared by two numbers. \\ \\
3. \textbf{LCM (Least Common Multiple):} The smallest multiple shared by two numbers. \\ \\
4. \textbf{Distributive Property:} Rewrite a sum using GCF. Example: $18 + 24 = 6(3 + 4)$. \\

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=4mm, bottom=4mm]
\textbf{Core Model: Why Do We Shift Decimals in Division?}

\medskip
Imagine trying to calculate how many groups of $0.2$ fit into $1.4$. Thinking in tiny pieces is hard! Instead, we can scale \textbf{both} numbers up by multiplying by 10 to make them friendly whole numbers. 

$$\frac{1.4}{0.2} \times \frac{10}{10} = \frac{14}{2}$$

Because we multiplied the top and bottom by the same amount, **$14 \div 2$ has the exact same answer as $1.4 \div 0.2$**. Shifting the decimal point is just a quick shortcut for multiplying by 10!

\medskip
\textbf{Example Problem: Calculate $13.775 \div 2.5$}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=4pt]
    \item \textbf{Setup the Long Division:} $2.5 \overline{)13.775}$
    \item \textbf{Make the Outside Number Whole:} Multiply by 10 by moving the decimal point \textbf{one place to the right}. $2.5 \rightarrow 25$.
    \item \textbf{Keep it Balanced:} Move the inside decimal point \textbf{one place to the right} as well. $13.775 \rightarrow 137.75$.
    \item \textbf{Divide Normally:} Pop the decimal point straight up into your answer line and run your long division algorithm with whole numbers:
    $$25 \overline{)137.75} = \mathbf{5.51}$$
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{4mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Worked Example:}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\medskip
\noindent
Find the GCF and LCM of 12 and 18, then write their sum $12 + 18$ using the Distributive Property.\\ 


\textbf{Solution:} 

Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. \\ Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18. \\ \textbf{GCF = 6}.\\ 
Multiples of 12: 12, 24, 36, 48. \\ Multiples of 18: 18, 36, 54. \\ \textbf{LCM = 36}.\\ 

Divide each original number by the GCF to get the inside numbers:
    $$\frac{12}{6} = 2 \qquad \text{and} \qquad \frac{18}{6} = 3$$

Distributive property: $12 + 18 = 6(2 + 3)$.
\end{tcolorbox}
