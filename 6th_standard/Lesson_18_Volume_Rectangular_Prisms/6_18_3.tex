% ==========================================================

% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=black!10]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Fractional Packing and Ratios}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Show your full calculations for these multi-step problems.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A rectangular container is $3\frac{1}{2}$ feet long, $2$ feet wide, and $1\frac{1}{2}$ feet high.\\ \\
\textbf{a)} What is the volume of the container?  \\ \\
\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{b)} If you fill it with small cubes that are $\frac{1}{2}$ foot on each side, how many cubes will you need? \\ \\
\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{c)} Show that the total volume of all the small cubes equals the volume of the container. 

\vspace{0.5cm}

\item A water tank is shaped like a rectangular prism. It is 5 meters long, 3 meters wide, and 2.5 meters tall. If the tank is currently filled to $\frac{4}{5}$ of its capacity, how many cubic meters of water are in the tank? 
\vspace{1cm}

\end{enumerate}

% ==========================================================
% PART B — FINDING MISSING DIMENSIONS
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=black!10]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Finding Missing Dimensions}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Set up and solve an equation for each missing dimension.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2.3cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A rectangular room has a volume of 960 ft$^3$. Its length is 16 ft and width is 12 ft. Find the ceiling height.

\vspace{2.2cm}

\item A cube has a volume of $\frac{27}{64}$ ft$^3$. Find the side length of the cube.

\vspace{2.2cm}

\item A prism has a square base and a volume of 200 cm$^3$. Its height is 8 cm. Find the side length of the base.

\vspace{2.5cm}

\end{enumerate}

% ==========================================================
% PART C — COMPARING VOLUMES
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=black!10]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Comparing and Reasoning About Volume}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Reason carefully and show all steps.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2.3cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item Box A has dimensions $4 \times 5 \times 6$ cm. Box B has dimensions $3 \times 8 \times 5$ cm. Which box has the greater volume? By how many cubic centimeters?

\vspace{5cm}

\item A prism has volume $V = l \times w \times h$. If you double only the height, what is the new volume in terms of $l$, $w$, and $h$? By what factor does the volume increase?

\vspace{2.5cm}

\end{enumerate}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=black!10]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Challenge Problems}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Solve these complex multi-step problems. Show all reasoning.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A warehouse has a floor that is 40 feet long and 25 feet wide. The ceiling is 15 feet high. Boxes measuring $2.5\text{ ft} \times 2\text{ ft} \times 1.5\text{ ft}$ need to be stacked from floor to ceiling. How many boxes can fit in the warehouse?

\vspace{2.3cm}

\item A swimming pool is rectangular: 30 m long, 12 m wide, and 2 m deep. The pool is currently $\frac{3}{4}$ full. How many more cubic meters of water are needed to fill it completely? If a pump fills at 3 cubic meters per minute, how long does it take?

\vspace{2.3cm}

\item A manufacturer ships product in boxes of two different sizes. Small boxes: $\frac{1}{2}\text{ ft} \times \frac{1}{2}\text{ ft} \times 1\text{ ft}$. Large boxes: $1\text{ ft} \times 1\text{ ft} \times 1\text{ ft}$. A shipping crate is $4\text{ ft} \times 4\text{ ft} \times 3\text{ ft}$. If the crate is packed with 24 large boxes, how many small boxes can fill the remaining space?

\vspace{2.5cm}

\end{enumerate}
