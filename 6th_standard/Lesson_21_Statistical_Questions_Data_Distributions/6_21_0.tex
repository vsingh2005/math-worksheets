\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=black!10]
    \large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Statistical Questions and Distribution Characteristics (Foundation)}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{2mm}

\noindent
1. \textbf{Statistical Question:} A question that anticipates variability in the data (i.e., there is not just one single fixed answer). \\
- \textit{Non-statistical:} ``How many sides does a triangle have?'' $\rightarrow$ (Exactly 3, no variance) \\
- \textit{Statistical:} ``How many minutes do students spend on homework?'' $\rightarrow$ (Answers will vary) \\ \\
\vspace{0.2cm}
2. \textbf{Data Distribution Summaries:} 
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0.3cm, leftmargin=0.5cm]
    \item \textbf{Center (Typical Value):} Calculated as the \textbf{Mean} (sum of data points divided by count: $\mu = \frac{\sum x_i}{n}$) or the \textbf{Median} (the exact middle value when values are ordered chronologically).
    \item \textbf{Spread (Variability):} Measures how scattered values are. The simplest index is the $\text{Range} = \text{Maximum} - \text{Minimum}$.
    \item \textbf{Shape:} Described by how data clusters along a horizontal number line axis.
\end{itemize}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=4mm, bottom=4mm]
\textbf{Worked Example:}
Identify if ``How many books did each student read last month?'' is statistical, and predict its shape if most read 1--2 books but one student read 20.\\
\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{Solution:}
It is a \textbf{statistical question} because reading habits vary among students. Since the majority of data points sit at small values (1--2) with a solitary high point pulling a long tail out to 20, the distribution shape is \textbf{skewed right}.
\end{tcolorbox}
