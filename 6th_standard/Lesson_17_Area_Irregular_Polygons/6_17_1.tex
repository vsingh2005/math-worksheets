\vspace{4mm}

% ==========================================================
% PART A — SIMPLE DECOMPOSITION
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=black!10]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Simple Decomposition}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Calculate the area of each shape. Assume all corners are right angles. Show the rectangles or triangles you used.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1.5cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item An L-shaped block: vertical stem $12\text{ in} \times 4\text{ in}$, horizontal foot $6\text{ in} \times 3\text{ in}$ extending to the right. Find the total area.

\vspace{2cm}

\item A T-shaped block: horizontal top $8\text{ cm} \times 2\text{ cm}$, vertical leg $6\text{ cm} \times 2\text{ cm}$ centered underneath. Find the total area.

\vspace{2cm}

\item A rectangle $10\text{ m} \times 8\text{ m}$ with a corner rectangle $3\text{ m} \times 4\text{ m}$ cut out of one corner. Find the remaining area.

\vspace{2cm}

\end{enumerate}

Find the area of each polygon described by its vertices. Draw a sketch and identify the shapes.

\vspace{2cm}

\item Vertices: $(-2, -2)$, $(2, -2)$, $(2, 3)$, $(-2, 3)$. \quad Area $=$ \blank

\vspace{2cm}

\item Vertices: $(0, 0)$, $(6, 0)$, $(6, 3)$, $(3, 3)$, $(3, 5)$, $(0, 5)$. Decompose into two rectangles.

\vspace{2cm}

\end{enumerate}

\newpage

% ==========================================================
% PART C — MIXED SHAPES
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=black!10]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Mixed Shape Area Practice}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Find the area of each figure. Label your work clearly.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A composite shape is made of a square ($5\text{ cm} \times 5\text{ cm}$) with a right triangle (base $= 5\text{ cm}$, height $= 3\text{ cm}$) sitting on top. Find the total area.

\vspace{2.2cm}

\item A U-shaped figure has an outer rectangle of $10\text{ ft} \times 8\text{ ft}$ and a rectangular hole of $4\text{ ft} \times 5\text{ ft}$ cut from the bottom center. Find the remaining area.

\vspace{2.2cm}

\item A right triangle has legs of 6 m and 8 m. Using the subtraction method (bounding rectangle), verify that the triangle's area equals $\frac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 8$.

\vspace{2.5cm}

\end{enumerate}

\newpage

% ==========================================================
% PART D — WORD PROBLEMS
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=black!10]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Real-World Area Problems}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Solve. Show all steps and include units.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2.4cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A garden bed is shaped like a large rectangle ($15\text{ ft} \times 10\text{ ft}$) with a smaller rectangle ($4\text{ ft} \times 5\text{ ft}$) cut out from one corner for a birdbath. What is the total planting area?

\vspace{2.3cm}

\item A tile design is L-shaped. It has a vertical section $3\text{ in} \times 8\text{ in}$ and a horizontal section $6\text{ in} \times 3\text{ in}$ at the bottom. If each tile costs \$0.25, and you need 20 tiles, what is the total area covered and the total cost?

\vspace{2.3cm}

\item A school hallway is 30 feet long and 8 feet wide, with a square alcove ($4\text{ ft} \times 4\text{ ft}$) built into one side. What is the total floor area of the hallway including the alcove?

\vspace{2.5cm}

\end{enumerate}
