\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
    \large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Mastery: Complex Ratio Problems}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
You have mastered identifying, writing, and scaling ratios. Now solve complex multi-step problems where you must interpret ratios and reason about quantities.

\vspace{4mm}

% ==========================================================
% PART A — INTERPRET AND USE PART-TO-WHOLE
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Part-to-Whole Reasoning}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Use part-to-whole ratios to solve.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A school library has 600 books. The ratio of fiction to all books is 3:5. How many fiction books are there?

\vspace{1.8cm}

\item In a survey, the ratio of people who prefer cats to all people is 2:7. If 70 people were surveyed, how many prefer cats?

\vspace{1.8cm}

\item A team has 24 players. The ratio of sixth graders to all players is 5:8. How many sixth graders are on the team?

\vspace{1.8cm}

\end{enumerate}

\newpage

% ==========================================================
% PART B — MULTI-STEP RATIO PROBLEMS
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Multi-Step Reasoning}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Solve using multiple ratio steps.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2.2cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A garden has tomato plants and pepper plants in ratio 3:2. There are 15 tomato plants. How many pepper plants are there? What is the total number of plants?

\vspace{2cm}

\item A paint mixture uses blue to yellow in ratio 2:5. You need 14 cans total. How many cans of each color?

\vspace{2cm}

\item A school has 8th graders and 6th graders in ratio 5:4. If there are 120 students total, how many are 6th graders and how many are 8th graders?

\vspace{2cm}

\end{enumerate}

\newpage

% ==========================================================
% PART C — COMPARE RATIO SITUATIONS
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Compare Ratios}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Compare different ratio situations. Show your work.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2.2cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item School A: Ratio of boys to girls is 2:3.\\
School B: Ratio of boys to girls is 3:4.\\
If each school has 20 students total (School A has 20, School B has 20), which school has more boys?

\vspace{2cm}

\item Recipe 1: Sugar to flour is 1:4. Recipe 2: Sugar to flour is 2:5. Which recipe is sweeter (more sugar relative to flour)?

\vspace{2cm}

\item Store A: Red shirts to blue shirts ratio is 3:7 (50 total shirts). Store B: Red to blue ratio is 4:6 (60 total shirts). Which store has more red shirts?

\vspace{2cm}

\end{enumerate}

\newpage

% ==========================================================
% PART D — CHALLENGE REASONING
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Challenge Problems}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Solve complex problems. Explain your thinking.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2.3cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A aquarium has fish in three colors. Blue to red is 2:3. Red to yellow is 3:4. If there are 24 blue fish, how many of each color?

\vspace{2.5cm}

\item A team roster: Athletes to coaches is 12:1. If there are 4 coaches, how many athletes? If half the athletes are on the field at a time, how many are on the field?

\vspace{2.5cm}

\item A survey shows: Dogs to cats owned is 5:3. If 240 people have either a dog or cat (no one has both), how many have dogs? How many have cats?

\vspace{2.5cm}

\end{enumerate}
