Solve each multi-step surface area problem. Show all work.

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.5cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A chocolate bar is packaged in a triangular prism box. The triangular ends have a base of 4 cm and a height of 3 cm. The other two sides of the triangle are 3.6 cm each. The length of the box is 15 cm.\\
\textbf{a)} Draw a rough net of the box.\\

\vspace{2.5cm}

\textbf{b)} Find the total surface area of cardboard needed. \blanklong

\vspace{0.5cm}

\item A company designs a cereal box with dimensions $8\text{ in} \times 2\text{ in} \times 11\text{ in}$.\\
\textbf{a)} How much cardboard is needed for one box? \\
\vspace{2cm}

\textbf{b)} A new design measures $7\text{ in} \times 3\text{ in} \times 10\text{ in}$. Which design uses less material? \

\vspace{1.5cm}

\end{enumerate}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=black!10]

\noindent
Solve each problem. Show all steps.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A shipping company packages items in boxes that are $2\text{ ft} \times 2\text{ ft} \times 3\text{ ft}$. How many square feet of cardboard are needed for 50 boxes?

\vspace{2.2cm}

\item A storage shed is shaped like a rectangular prism: $12\text{ ft} \times 8\text{ ft} \times 7\text{ ft}$. The shed needs paint on all exterior surfaces (no floor). If one gallon covers 30 square feet, how many gallons are needed?

\vspace{2.5cm}

\end{enumerate}

\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Reasoning About Surface Area}}

\noindent
Think carefully and explain your reasoning.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2.3cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A cube and a rectangular prism both have the same volume of $27\text{ cm}^3$. The cube has side length 3 cm. The rectangular prism has dimensions $1\text{ cm} \times 3\text{ cm} \times 9\text{ cm}$. Compare their surface areas. Which shape uses less material?

\vspace{5cm}

\item A square pyramid has a base side of $s$ and a slant height of $l$. Write the formula for its total surface area. If the base side doubles but the slant height stays the same, how does the surface area change? Explain.

\vspace{2.5cm}

\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2.4cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A play tent is shaped like a triangular prism. Its triangular cross-section is an isosceles triangle with base 3 m and equal sides of 2.5 m each (height $\approx 2\text{ m}$). The tent is 4 m long. The floor is not fabric. Find the total fabric area needed.

\vspace{2.3cm}

\item A decorative wooden box has no lid. Its outer dimensions are $10\text{ in} \times 6\text{ in} \times 4\text{ in}$. If the wood is $\frac{1}{2}$ in thick on all sides and bottom, what are the inner dimensions? What is the inner surface area?

\vspace{2.3cm}

\item A gift shop wraps boxes in paper. The boxes are cubes with side $= 6\text{ in}$. Wrapping paper is sold in rolls that are 24 in wide and 100 in long. How many boxes can be wrapped with one roll? (Assume no overlap needed.)

\end{enumerate}
