% ==========================================================
% SECTION 1: WHAT IS A RATIO?
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
    \large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Understanding Ratios (Foundation for Unit Rates)}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
A \textbf{ratio} compares two quantities. For example:

\begin{itemize}
    \item ``There are 3 apples for every 2 oranges'' — written as \textbf{3:2} or $\dfrac{3}{2}$
    \item ``A car travels 120 miles in 2 hours'' — written as \textbf{120:2} or $\dfrac{120}{2}$
    \item ``A recipe needs 2 cups flour for every 1 cup sugar'' — written as \textbf{2:1} or $\dfrac{2}{1}$
\end{itemize}

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Key Idea:} In all these ratios, we're comparing \textbf{two different things} (apples to oranges, miles to hours, flour to sugar).

\vspace{1cm}

% ==========================================================
% SECTION 2: WHAT IS A UNIT RATE?
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{What is a Unit Rate?}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
A \textbf{unit rate} is a special ratio where the \textbf{second quantity is 1}. 

\begin{itemize}
    \item If there are 3 apples for every 2 oranges, the unit rate is $\dfrac{3}{2} = 1.5$ apples \textbf{per orange}
    \item If a car travels 120 miles in 2 hours, the unit rate is $\dfrac{120}{2} = 60$ miles \textbf{per hour}
    \item If a recipe needs 2 cups flour for 1 cup sugar, the unit rate is $\dfrac{2}{1} = 2$ cups flour \textbf{per cup sugar}
\end{itemize}

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{The key word: ``per'' means ``for every 1 of''} \\
\textbf{Unit rates tell us about a single item or unit.}

\vspace{3mm}

% ==========================================================
% WORKED EXAMPLE 1
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Worked Example 1: Finding Unit Rate from a Simple Ratio}

\medskip

\textbf{Problem:} A grocery store sells 4 bananas for \$2. What is the unit price (price per banana)?

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Solution:}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Write the ratio: $\dfrac{\$2}{4 \text{ bananas}}$
    
    \item To find the unit rate, divide both the top and bottom by the bottom number:
    
    $\dfrac{\$2}{4 \text{ bananas}} = \dfrac{\$2 \div 4}{4 \div 4} = \dfrac{\$0.50}{1 \text{ banana}}$
    
    \item The unit rate is \textbf{\$0.50 per banana} or \textbf{\$0.50 for each banana}.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Check:} If each banana costs \$0.50, then 4 bananas cost $4 \times \$0.50 = \$2$ 

\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{4mm}

% ==========================================================
% SECTION 3: SPEED — A SPECIAL UNIT RATE
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Speed: Comparing Distance and Time}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
\textbf{Speed} is a unit rate that compares \textbf{distance} to \textbf{time}. Common speed units include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Miles per hour (mph)
    \item Kilometers per hour (km/h)
    \item Meters per second (m/s)
    \item Feet per second (ft/s)
\end{itemize}

\medskip

\noindent
The relationship between distance, rate (speed), and time is:

$$\boxed{\text{Distance} = \text{Speed} \times \text{Time}}$$

\noindent
This can be rearranged:
$$\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}} \quad \text{and} \quad \text{Time} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Speed}}$$

\vspace{4mm}

% ==========================================================
% WORKED EXAMPLE 2
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Worked Example 2: Finding Speed}

\medskip

\textbf{Problem:} A runner completes 6 miles in 1 hour. What is her speed in miles per hour?

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Solution:}

\medskip

\noindent
Speed $= \dfrac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}} = \dfrac{6 \text{ miles}}{1 \text{ hour}} = 6$ mph

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Interpretation:} The runner travels 6 miles every hour.

\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{1mm}

% ==========================================================
% WORKED EXAMPLE 3
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Worked Example 3: Using Speed to Find Distance}

\medskip

\textbf{Problem:} A car travels at 60 mph for 3 hours. How far does it go?

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Solution:}

\medskip

\noindent
Distance $= \text{Speed} \times \text{Time} = 60 \text{ mph} \times 3 \text{ hours} = 180$ miles

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Interpretation:} At 60 miles per hour, after 3 hours the car has traveled 180 miles total.

\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Why Unit Rates Matter: Making Comparisons}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Unit rates help us compare things fairly. Imagine you're at a store and see:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Option A: 8 notebooks for \$12
    \item Option B: 6 notebooks for \$9
\end{itemize}

\noindent
Which is the better deal? \textbf{Without unit rates, this is hard to tell.} But with unit rates:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Option A: $\dfrac{\$12}{8} = \$1.50$ per notebook
    \item Option B: $\dfrac{\$9}{6} = \$1.50$ per notebook
\end{itemize}

\noindent
\textbf{They're the same price!} Unit rates let us compare fairly.

\vspace{4mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Unit Rates with Fractions}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Sometimes unit rates involve fractions. For example, when time or quantity is given as a fraction.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Worked Example 4: Unit Rate with a Fraction}

\medskip

\textbf{Problem:} A worker paints 15 square meters in $\dfrac{1}{3}$ hour. What is the painting rate in square meters per hour?

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Solution:}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Write the ratio: $\dfrac{15 \text{ sq m}}{\frac{1}{3} \text{ hour}}$
    
    \item To find the unit rate, divide by the fraction. Remember: divide by a fraction means multiply by its reciprocal.
    
    $\dfrac{15}{\frac{1}{3}} = 15 \times \dfrac{3}{1} = 15 \times 3 = 45$ square meters per hour
    
    \item The unit rate is \textbf{45 square meters per hour}.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Check:} If the rate is 45 sq m per hour, then in $\dfrac{1}{3}$ hour: $45 \times \dfrac{1}{3} = 15$ sq m 

\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{4mm}
