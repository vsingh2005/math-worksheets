% SECTION 1: CONVERSION AS A RATIO
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
    \large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Conversion as a Ratio (Foundation)}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
To convert between units, we use a \textbf{conversion ratio} or \textbf{conversion factor}. This is a ratio that equals 1 because the units are equal.

\vspace{0.8cm}

\begin{itemize}
    \item 12 inches = 1 foot, so the ratio $\dfrac{12 \text{ inches}}{1 \text{ foot}} = 1$
    \item 5,280 feet = 1 mile, so the ratio $\dfrac{5,280 \text{ feet}}{1 \text{ mile}} = 1$
    \item 100 centimeters = 1 meter, so the ratio $\dfrac{100 \text{ cm}}{1 \text{ m}} = 1$
\end{itemize}

\vspace{1cm}

\noindent
\textbf{Key Idea:} Multiply by a conversion ratio to change units without changing the actual measurement.

\vspace{1cm}

% CUSTOMARY UNITS
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!50, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Customary Units (inches, feet, miles, pounds, etc.)}

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Length:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item 12 inches (in) = 1 foot (ft)
    \item 3 feet = 1 yard (yd)
    \item 5,280 feet = 1 mile (mi)
\end{itemize}

\noindent
\textbf{Weight:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item 16 ounces (oz) = 1 pound (lb)
\end{itemize}

\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{4mm}

% ==========================================================
% WORKED EXAMPLE 1
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Worked Example 1: Convert Feet to Inches}

\medskip

\textbf{Problem:} Convert 5 feet to inches.

\medskip

\noindent

\vspace{1cm}
\textbf{Solution:}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Identify the conversion ratio: $\dfrac{12 \text{ inches}}{1 \text{ foot}}$
    \vspace{1cm}
    \item Multiply: $5 \text{ feet} \times \dfrac{12 \text{ inches}}{1 \text{ foot}} = \dfrac{5 \times 12}{1} \text{ inches} = 60 \text{ inches}$
    \vspace{1cm}
    \item Answer: 5 feet = \textbf{60 inches}
\end{enumerate}

\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Worked Example 1B: Convert Small Units to Large Units (Division)}

\medskip

\textbf{Problem:} Convert 48 ounces to pounds.

\medskip
\vspace{1cm}

\noindent
\textbf{Solution:}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Identify the conversion ratio: 16 ounces = 1 pound. Since we are moving from a smaller unit to a larger unit, we will \textbf{divide}.
    \vspace{1cm}
    
    \item Set up the fraction: $48 \text{ ounces} \times \dfrac{1 \text{ lb}}{16 \text{ oz}} = \dfrac{48}{16} \text{ lbs}$
    \vspace{1cm}
    
    \item Multiply/Divide: $48 \div 16 = 3$
    \vspace{1cm}
    
    \item Answer: 48 ounces = \textbf{3 pounds}
    
\end{enumerate}

\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{4mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Metric System}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
The metric system is based on powers of 10, making conversions easier.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!50, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Metric Conversions}

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Length:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item 10 millimeters (mm) = 1 centimeter (cm)
    \item 100 centimeters = 1 meter (m)
    \item 1,000 meters = 1 kilometer (km)
\end{itemize}
\vspace{1cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Mass/Weight:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item 1,000 grams (g) = 1 kilogram (kg)
\end{itemize}
\vspace{1cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Capacity:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item 1,000 milliliters (mL) = 1 liter (L)
\end{itemize}

\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{0.5cm}

% ==========================================================
% WORKED EXAMPLE 2
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Worked Example 2: Convert Meters to Centimeters}

\medskip

\textbf{Problem:} Convert 4 meters to centimeters.

\medskip

\noindent

\vspace{1cm}
\textbf{Solution:}

\medskip

\noindent
$4 \text{ m} \times \dfrac{100 \text{ cm}}{1 \text{ m}} = 4 \times 100 \text{ cm} = 400 \text{ cm}$

\medskip
\vspace{1cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Answer:} 4 meters = \textbf{400 centimeters}

\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=customteal!20]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Converting Rates and Speeds}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
When converting a rate (like speed or flow), apply the conversion factor directly to the top or bottom unit.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]

\textbf{Worked Example 3: Converting Metric Rates}

\medskip

\textbf{Problem:} A water pump dispenses 3,000 milliliters of water every minute. How many liters does it dispense per minute?

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Solution:}
$$\dfrac{3,000 \text{ mL}}{1 \text{ minute}} \times \dfrac{1 \text{ L}}{1,000 \text{ mL}} = \dfrac{3,000}{1,000} \text{ L per minute} = 3 \text{ Liters/minute}$$



\end{tcolorbox}
