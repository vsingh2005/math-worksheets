\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=black!10]
    \large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Writing and Evaluating Algebraic Expressions (Foundation)}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3mm}

\noindent
1. \textbf{Writing Expressions:} Translate word descriptions into mathematical terms. \\
- Key words: \textbf{Sum/Plus} (+), \textbf{Difference/Less than} (-), \textbf{Product/Times} ($\times$), \textbf{Quotient/Ratio} ($/$, $\div$). \\
- \textit{Important:} ``$b$ less than $a$'' translates to $a - b$, NOT $b - a$. \\
2. \textbf{Evaluating Expressions:} Substitute variable values into the expression, then simplify.

\vspace{4mm}

\small
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=blue!5, colframe=blue!60!black, boxrule=0.8pt]
\textbf{The Rule of Invisible Multiplication (Juxtaposition)}

\medskip
In algebra, multiplication symbols ($\times$) are usually omitted between a number and a variable, or between two variables. 
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
    \item $6y$ means \textbf{$6$ times $y$} \quad $\rightarrow$ \quad If $y = 3$, then $6y = 6 \times 3 = 18$ (NOT $63$).
    \item $xy$ means \textbf{$x$ times $y$} \quad $\rightarrow$ \quad If $x = 4$ and $y = 3$, then $xy = 4 \times 3 = 12$.
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{2mm}
\vspace{1cm}
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!25, boxrule=0.6pt, arc=4mm, left=6mm, right=6mm, top=5mm, bottom=5mm]
\textbf{Worked Example:}

\medskip
\noindent
\vspace{0.2cm}

Evaluate the expression $2x^2 - 3y$ for $x = 3$ and $y = 2$.\\
\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{Solution:} \\
1) Substitute: $2(3)^2 - 3(2)$. \\ \\
2) Exponents: $3^2 = 9$. \\ \\
3) Multiplications: $2 \times 9 - 3 \times 2 = 18 - 6$. \\ \\
4) Subtract: $18 - 6 = 12$. \\
\vspace{0.5cm}

\end{tcolorbox}
