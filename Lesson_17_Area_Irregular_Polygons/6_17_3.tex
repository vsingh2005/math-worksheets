% ==========================================================
% PART A — ADVANCED COMPOSITE DESIGNS
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=black!10]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Advanced Composite Designs}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Analyze each figure and find the total area. Show all decomposition steps.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2.2cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A house floor plan is composed of three rooms: a living room ($20\text{ ft} \times 15\text{ ft}$), a kitchen ($10\text{ ft} \times 12\text{ ft}$), and a triangular patio with a base of 12 ft and a height of 8 ft. Find the total area of the floor plan.

\vspace{2cm}

\item If flooring costs \$4.50 per square foot, what is the cost to floor only the living room and kitchen from Problem 1?

\vspace{2cm}

\item A coordinate grid map shows a park with vertices at $P(-3, -2)$, $Q(5, -2)$, $R(5, 4)$, $S(1, 4)$, $T(1, 1)$, $U(-3, 1)$. Decompose the park into two rectangles and find the total area.

\vspace{2cm}

\end{enumerate}

% ==========================================================
% PART B — REAL-WORLD DESIGN
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=black!10]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Real-World Design Problems}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Solve each scenario. Show all steps.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2.3cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A playground has a rectangular area ($30\text{ m} \times 20\text{ m}$) with a sandpit cut out ($6\text{ m} \times 6\text{ m}$) and a triangular garden ($\text{base} = 8\text{ m}$, $\text{height} = 5\text{ m}$) in one corner. What is the remaining play area?

\vspace{2.2cm}

\item An architect designs a lobby shaped like a large rectangle ($18\text{ ft} \times 14\text{ ft}$) with two identical rectangular alcoves ($3\text{ ft} \times 4\text{ ft}$ each) built into opposite walls. Find the total floor area.

\vspace{2.2cm}

\item A decorative window pane is $4\text{ ft} \times 6\text{ ft}$. It has six square panes ($1\text{ ft} \times 1\text{ ft}$ each) cut out. Find the total glass area remaining.

\vspace{2.5cm}

\end{enumerate}

% ==========================================================
% PART C — REASONING AND EXPLANATION
% ==========================================================
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=black!10]
\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Reasoning About Irregular Area}}
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent
Explain your reasoning for each question.

\vspace{3mm}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2.3cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item Two students decompose the same irregular polygon in different ways. Student A gets an area of 84 cm$^2$ and Student B gets 84 cm$^2$ as well. Does the method of decomposition affect the final area? Explain why or why not.

\vspace{2.2cm}

\item A rectangle with dimensions $a \times b$ has a triangle with base $a$ and height $\frac{b}{2}$ cut from its top. Write an algebraic expression for the remaining area. Simplify your expression.

\vspace{2.5cm}

\end{enumerate}

\large\textcolor{black}{\textbf{Challenge Problems}}

\noindent
Solve these complex multi-step problems. Show all reasoning.

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2.4cm, leftmargin=0.6cm]

\item A city block is an irregular hexagon with vertices at $(0, 0)$, $(10, 0)$, $(14, 6)$, $(10, 12)$, $(0, 12)$, and $(-4, 6)$. Decompose the hexagon into a rectangle and two triangles to estimate the total area.

\vspace{2.3cm}

\item A homeowner wants to paint all the wall surfaces of an L-shaped room. One section is $12\text{ ft} \times 9\text{ ft}$ and the other section is $8\text{ ft} \times 6\text{ ft}$. Both sections have 8-foot ceilings. There are two rectangular doors ($3\text{ ft} \times 7\text{ ft}$ each) and one square window ($2\text{ ft} \times 2\text{ ft}$). Find the total paintable wall area.

\vspace{2.3cm}

\item Design your own composite figure using at least three shapes. State the dimensions of each part, calculate the total area, and write a real-world context (e.g., a room, a garden, a sign) that the figure could represent.

\vspace{2.5cm}

\end{enumerate}
